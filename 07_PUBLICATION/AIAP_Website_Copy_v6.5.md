# Artificial Intelligence Assurance Protocol (AIAP)

## From task design to claim-level assurance

AIAP asks a governing question that every consequential assessment should be able to answer:

> **What does this assessment allow an institution to claim about this student?**

Generative AI has made a longstanding assessment weakness impossible to ignore. A polished submitted product may show that a strong artifact exists, but it does not automatically show what the enrolled student can personally explain, defend, verify, adapt, or transfer. Nor does a successful oral check reconstruct every part of the production history. AIAP prevents these different evidence objects from being silently treated as interchangeable.

## The four assurance routes

**Lane 1 - Secured.** The student demonstrates essential capability under materially constrained conditions. The inference remains bounded by identity, construct validity, accessibility, control strength, and residual threats.

**Lane 2A - Competence-authenticated open.** Students may complete complex work in open conditions, including permitted AI use, but a claim-targeted authentication event tests present competence through explanation, defence, verification, adaptation, or transfer. Consequential use includes at least one unseen or meaningfully perturbed element.

**Lane 2B - Open non-certifying.** The activity supports practice, feedback, or triangulation but does not carry primary certification authority for the specified capability. The programme must show where that capability is assured elsewhere.

**Lane 3 - AI-integrated open.** Responsible AI-mediated practice is part of the construct. Students demonstrate tool choice, verification, correction, rejected recommendations, retained human decisions, stop/override points, risk controls, and responsibility. High-consequence oversight claims require authenticated judgement or transfer, not logs alone.

## What AIAP adds

AIAP is not another permission scale. It connects:

**claim -> condition -> assurance route -> evidence -> combination rule -> inference -> programme/public use**

The complete release includes a canonical paper, a Normative Assurance Standard, implementation handbook, student and instructor toolkit, role-coding manual, data-protection/accessibility/evidence-governance guide, pilot protocol, pilot-readiness pack, programme map, workload and feasibility models, prompt-security instruments, a JSON Schema, worked examples, and a semantic validator.

## The fail-closed boundary

AIAP does not allow a favourable average to compensate for a failed rights, identity, construct, accessibility, security, independence, governance, or authority prerequisite. Its Assurance Capacity Gate contains thirteen conditions, from claim mapping and prompt security through appeals, data governance, threat modelling, and capacity-degradation fallbacks.

## Scientific status

AIAP v6.5 is specification-complete for scholarly evaluation and governed pilot preparation. It is **not empirically validated as an integrated institutional intervention**. The empirical programme is sequenced. **Phase 1** is a bounded feasibility and measurement study, approximately 120 students across two or three modules, with sample size determined by the primary estimand and precision target. **Phase 2** expands to at least two and preferably three or more institutions and at least two academic cycles. Both phases test reliability, generalization, incremental validity, equity, privacy, security, workload, and decision utility. Null and adverse findings are designed to narrow, redesign, or withdraw claims rather than be hidden.
