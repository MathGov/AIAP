Generative artificial intelligence has weakened a common assessment inference: that an unsupervised submitted product reliably evidences the capability of the enrolled student. This methodological paper develops the Artificial Intelligence Assurance Protocol (AIAP), a claim-level architecture for aligning learning claims, evidence-producing conditions, assurance mechanisms, evidence, decision rules, and programme or public uses. AIAP decomposes composite assessments into material claims and assigns one of four routes: secured assessment, competence-authenticated open assessment, open non-certifying assessment, or AI-integrated assessment. The protocol separates artifact quality, present demonstrable competence, production provenance, and AI-integrated capability; requires predeclared product-authentication rules; treats modality and evidence dependence as validity conditions; and constrains degree and public claims to the evidence collected. It also specifies prompt-security and form-equivalence controls, a thirteen-condition fail-closed Assurance Capacity Gate, minimum appeal and accessibility protections, temporal requalification, programme-level assurance mapping, and a preregistration-ready validation protocol. A machine-readable record and semantic validator test cross-field coherence without conflating structural validity with substantive authority. AIAP is specification-complete for scholarly evaluation and governed pilot preparation, but it is not empirically validated as an integrated institutional intervention. Its central falsifiable proposition is that competence-authenticated open assessment can add enough reliable, generalizable, equitable, secure, and decision-relevant evidence to justify its privacy and workload costs.
