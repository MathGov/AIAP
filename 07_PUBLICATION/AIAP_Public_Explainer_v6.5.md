# AIAP in Plain Language

## The problem

A take-home paper can show that a good paper exists. In an AI-rich environment, it may not show everything a university wants to claim about the student who submitted it.

The same mistake happens in the opposite direction. A student who answers a few oral questions may demonstrate current understanding, but that does not prove who produced every sentence or every step of the original artifact.

AIAP keeps these claims separate.

## The core rule

Before an assessment is released, the programme states:

1. what capability is being claimed;
2. under what conditions the evidence will be produced;
3. which assurance route applies;
4. what evidence will be collected;
5. how different evidence sources affect the result;
6. what may and may not be inferred; and
7. how the result may be used in programme, degree, employer, accreditation, or public wording.

## Four routes, one discipline

- **Secure what must be personally demonstrated.**
- **Authenticate complex open work when present competence matters.**
- **Make non-certification explicit where a task is mainly for learning or feedback.**
- **Assess AI-integrated practice directly when responsible AI use is itself the capability.**

## What AIAP refuses to pretend

- A strong product is not automatically independent capability.
- A viva is not automatically proof of authorship.
- Workflow logs are not automatically accountable oversight.
- A programme map is not individual certification.
- A completed framework is not empirical validation.

## What happens next

The architecture is ready to be challenged in governed pilots. Phase 1 is a bounded feasibility and measurement study; Phase 2 expands to a multi-institution comparison over at least two academic cycles. The tests focus on whether Lane 2A authentication is reliable, generalizes across prompts and occasions, adds real decision value, remains equitable and accessible, resists realistic attacks, protects privacy, and justifies its burden.

AIAP is complete enough to be tested - and bounded enough to be changed when evidence demands it.
